# Planet Rescue

Defend your planet against a never-ending rain of asteroids: your weapons fire on their own, you steer the **Gravitron** to collect the debris, and every piece of material you gather goes into upgrades.

**[Play it in your browser](https://sylvainlano.github.io/PlanetRescue/index.html)**: no install, no build, plain HTML5 canvas and JavaScript.

## The game

Your planet, the Earth, sits in the middle of the screen and asteroids come from every direction. When one crashes on the planet, you lose as many million inhabitants as it had life left. If your population reaches zero, the game is over.

The asteroids get tougher as time passes, one more point of life each minute, and a new threat comes every two minutes, announced by the NAZA at the top of the screen. After two minutes, **comets**: small, fast and fragile, they often graze the planet, take only two million inhabitants when they hit, and their debris are ice, worth 4 mat each. After four minutes, **splitting asteroids**: bigger than the others, cracked, glowing red in a red halo beating softly, they burst into three small fast rocks when destroyed, so break them far from your planet. After six minutes, a few **giants** come along: dark rusty red rocks, bigger, heavier and slower, with four times the life of the others. A giant crashing on your planet takes all its life left away: destroy it first. After eight minutes, every minute or so, **swarms**: twelve small pebbles coming together from one side, announced with that side, marked by a red arrow at the edge of the screen, and with 60 mat your inhabitants give you to get ready (a shorter cooldown, the shield turned to them, the Spiral…). After ten minutes, every minute or so, groups of three **armored asteroids**: plated with steel, as an armor in a game: their plates absorb 3 of each shot's power, with a white spark, and a shot no stronger bounces off; critical shots go through whole, and the laser burns through. They are announced in the same way, with 80 mat to raise your fire power.

- **The Gravitron** is an arc around your planet. Its open part pulls debris in, which turns them into **mat** (material), but it also pulls asteroids toward you. It opens up to 60° at first; the Field Width upgrade opens it wider, to 90°, 150°, 240°, then the whole circle. Its closed part can brake them once you buy the Gravity Cushion upgrade. It can also become a shield and send out damaging pulses. The whole art is to gather debris without drawing the asteroids in: the Field Scanner upgrade shows the limits of the field, then which asteroids are pulled (red) or braked (green).
- **The weapons fire automatically.** You start with a basic cannon guided by its SMART AI, which goes up to level 5: it learns to aim ahead of its targets, then to choose them wisely. From level 2, it leaves the asteroids no shot can catch on their way (going away, or passing by too fast for its shots), ringed with grey dashes, and a mouse over one tells why; you can still choose one with T, and the cannon then shoots where its shot comes the closest. Faster shots, or a new way for the asteroid (a collision, the Gravitron, a kamikaze), make it worth a look again. You can also buy the Spiral & Orbital, the Harvester, the Cryo, the Magnetic and the Hammer & Broom weapons, one after the other: each one comes in the menu once the one before is bought. The Harvester strikes the asteroids in front of the Gravitron's opening at once with golden rays, weak, but each ray tears off two debris, falling right into the opening, or more with its Yield: turned toward the asteroids to mine them, the opening pulls them in too, unless the Sorting is high. The Cryo's ice shards fly to the asteroid nearest to the planet and freeze it for 5 seconds: stopped, out of the Gravitron's pull, a shock only making it slide a little; once thawed, it stays where it is, without any speed, until a shock or the Gravitron sends it flying again. The Hammer & Broom's waves, translucent, the sky rippling through them, damage a little each asteroid they cross and slow down the ones coming closer, before they fade; at first they leave through the Gravitron's opening, to slow down the asteroids it pulls in. The basic cannon always stays active; one of the others can be active next to it, two with the Multiple weapon upgrade.
- **Kamikazes** stop the asteroids your cannon can't catch. A click on an asteroid, or F for the one your cannon aims at, sends a small ship from your planet: it flies ahead of the asteroid, turns back and rams it head-on, which stops it (a heavy one less), for your cannon to hit it at last. One ship is ready at the start, and a new one is built in 30 seconds; the pause menu sells up to three of them, and a shorter building time.
- **The upgrade menu** opens with the pause. There you spend your mat on the Gravitron, the weapons and the planet: healing (5 mat per inhabitant, cheaper and faster with the Healing Factor, and up to three inhabitants rebuilt at a time with the Rebuilding Crews), maximum health, storage, auto-healing (an inhabitant healed for every few mat your Gravitron gathers, without spending them), and recovery of mat from crashed asteroids. Every upgrade is an illustrated tile showing what it is worth now (the damage of a shot, the shots per second, the inhabitants left to heal, the mat a healing costs…), framed in green when you can buy one more, in red when you lack mat, in gold at its maximum; its tooltip tells what a click buys and its price, and a click buys it. Each section and each weapon tab tells, in green, how many things you can buy there with your mat, or else the price of the cheapest one. Some upgrades only come once they are worth buying: the Gravitron Pulse with the shield, the Gravity Cushion with a stronger Gravity Generator, the Multiple weapon structure with a second weapon.
- **Luck is on your side, now and then.** One shot in twenty of the Basic, Spiral and Magnetic weapons is **critical**: red, bigger, it does three times its power, and flashes red on the asteroid it hits. Some asteroids carry a **metal** in their rock, shining in veins: green copper, silver or gold. Half of their debris are made of it and worth 2, 3 or 5 mat: aim at them, and open your Gravitron at the right time. The Main Structure's tab sells a higher Critical Chance (up to one shot in four) and, once 15% of the shots are critical, Critical Damage (up to five times the power), and the Gravitron's Prospector makes the metals up to twice as frequent.
- **Three difficulty levels** can be chosen in the start menu, which tells what each one changes. Easy gives your planet more life and mat, sends the asteroids more slowly and lets your cannon anticipate from the start. Hard sends them faster and faster, sooner.
- **The research tree** grows with your achievements: each one of its branches opens for good with an achievement. In a game, your planet first needs a **Research Lab**, bought with its upgrades once a branch is open (its level 2, the Automation, then validates each research once complete and starts the next one by itself); then a research takes time, not mat: start one from the pause menu, and validate it there once complete to get its bonus. Only one research goes on at a time; you can stop it and go back to it later, its time is kept. A note at the bottom right tells you when a research waits for you. The first branch brings the **Missile Launcher**, a secondary weapon whose missiles explode on the asteroid they touch, damaging the others around, and then the **Homing Missiles**, which steer toward their target. The second branch, opened by the Demolisher achievement, brings the **Nitroglycerine**: a slow capsule turns an asteroid far from your planet into a glowing bomb, which explodes when it touches another asteroid, when it is destroyed, or as soon as your planet comes into its blast, damaging every asteroid in it but sparing your planet. Steer it into the others with your Gravitron. Then the **Chain Reaction** turns the asteroids a blast doesn't destroy into bombs too, with half its power, until the reaction fades out. The third branch, opened by the Sharpshooter achievement, brings the **Laser**: its beam burns an asteroid for two seconds, then cools down; it aims first at the giants, then at the asteroids going to crash on your planet. Then the **Piercing Beam** goes through its target and spreads behind it in a cone of light to the edge of space, burning at half power every other asteroid it reaches. The fourth branch, opened by the Bank Shot achievement, brings the **Rebound Shot**: a fast shot that flies to the main cannon's target, then ricochets on the asteroids it hits (going a bit astray on their rough rock), on the edges of space and on your planet's atmosphere, which it spares, until it has made all its rebounds; every asteroid it hits takes its power. Then the **Trick Shot** reckons the ricochets before each shot: among 72 angles around the planet, it takes the one whose way hits the most asteroids, foreseeing their moves, and its shots no longer go astray. The fifth branch, opened by the Collector achievement, is for your **Gravitron**, and each of its researches waits for an achievement of its own: the **Wide Net** (Collector) widens its field by 20 degrees on each side for the debris only, drawn dotted around the opening; the **Compactor** (Hoarder) brings an upgrade of the same name, bought up to three times, each level making 20% more mat from the debris; the **Gravity Brake** (Exterminator) slows down the asteroids in the opening, the heavy ones less; and the **Slingshot** (Bank Shot) makes an asteroid the Gravitron has turned from its way hit twice as hard the asteroids it runs into. The sixth branch, opened by the Untouchable achievement, is for your **planet**, each research waiting for its own achievement as well: the **Thick Atmosphere** (Untouchable) burns 2 points of life off every asteroid coming into it, with a blue glow around the planet and flames on the asteroids, and the weakest ones burn up; the **Early Warning** (Veteran) brings an upgrade of the same name, bought up to three times, each level evacuating 10% of the inhabitants a crash would take away; and the **Shelters** (Legend), once in a game, save 10% of your inhabitants when a crash should end it. The secondary weapons have their own row of tabs in the pause menu.
- **25 achievements** reward your feats, from your first destroyed asteroid to six minutes of survival, an asteroid you sent into another one with your Gravitron, a giant finished by a ricochet, or 8 researches completed in a game. Each one is a bronze, silver or gold medal, announced at the bottom of the game area when you get it. The Achievements page of the start menu shows your medals; the others stay secret until you get them. They are saved in your browser, whatever the difficulty level.
- At the end of a game, the Game Over screen shows your score and its details, your numbers (survival time, asteroids and giants destroyed, mat gathered, accuracy, hits taken and inhabitants lost, upgrades), the records you beat, the medals of the achievements you got, and a tip from the NAZA, chosen from what happened in the game, with the number behind it lit up. The hi-scores of each difficulty level are saved in your browser, and the hi-scores page of the start menu shows them, one tab per level, where each level's hi-scores can be erased. The score is 10 points per asteroid destroyed (50 per giant), plus the mat gathered, plus the seconds survived.

### Controls

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| ← → (or A, D; Q, D on an AZERTY keyboard) | rotate the Gravitron |
| ↑ (or W; Z on an AZERTY keyboard) | shorten the Gravitron's aperture |
| ↓ (or S) | widen the Gravitron's aperture |
| Space | pause and open the upgrade menu |
| Delete (or T, for Target) | change the cannon's target |
| End (or R, for Reset) | clear the target selection |
| Click on an asteroid, or F for the circled one | send a kamikaze |
| M | sound on or off (off at first) |

Click the status bar at the top to switch between bars and digits. The letters are read by their place on the keyboard: W A S D on a QWERTY keyboard are Z Q S D on an AZERTY one, and the Infos page shows the letters of your own keyboard.

## History

Planet Rescue was made in **2010** by [Sylvain Lano](https://sylvainlano.github.io/). It was Sylvain's first real JavaScript game: until then, Sylvain's projects were whole PHP websites with Flash inside them. The HTML5 canvas was still brand new.

The project first lived on [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/projects/planetrescue/), in a Subversion repository. The hardest part of the code was computing trajectories and **meeting points between moving objects**, so that the cannon could aim where an asteroid *will be*. The equation was worked out with the help of Marc35 from the [ilephysique.net](https://www.ilephysique.net/) forum. It worked in about 80 % of the cases, never quite all of them, and the game stopped at version 0.4. Its to-do list (below) was full of ideas that never got coded.

In **2023** the code moved to GitHub, almost untouched.

In **2026** the game came back to life. The goals are to keep its spirit, make it fairer (it forgives nothing), finish what was never finished, and modernize it step by step.

## Roadmap (2026)

The order may change as we go.

1. **Fairer difficulty:** difficulty levels (done in 0.7.0), the Field Scanner (done in 0.8.0), and SMART AI levels 3 to 5 as designed in 2010 (done in 0.9.0).
2. **Modernization:** upgrades described as data, a pause menu without jQuery (ready for the research tree), lighter assets (all done in 0.10.1).
3. **Graphics:** rocky asteroids (done in 0.11.0) and the Earth as your planet (done in 0.12.0), both drawn at the screen's resolution, a smooth animation (done in 0.12.1) and twinkling stars (done in 0.13.0), then a hand-made animated background.
4. **Touch screens:** given up. Playing without a keyboard would take simpler Gravitron controls, which would change the game too much.
5. **The 2010 ideas:** achievements (done in 0.14.0), then the research tree (started in 0.15.0 with its first branch, the missiles, then the explosives in 0.25.0, the lasers in 0.26.0 and the ricochets in 0.32.0; each branch has two researches since 0.33.0), new secondary weapons (all four of 2010 done in 0.32.0), then the free-play mode, which became the Adventure (in progress: its first three levels are in the code, hidden until it is ready, and playable by opening the game as `index.html?adventure`; to come: a map of the levels, more threats and a boss; it will be version 1.0.0).

## Under the hood

No build step and no dependencies to install: open `index.html` in a browser and play.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | page and menus |
| `css/main.css` | layout and look |
| `src/main.js` | start-up: canvas, zoom, game loop (25 turns a second, drawn at each refresh of the screen), keyboard |
| `src/engine.js` | the game's turn and its states (menu, tutorial, countdown, playing, hi-scores), with the Arcade's asteroids coming on a timer, and `drawGame`, which draws them |
| `src/functions.js` | debris, pulses, Gravitron, weapons, SMART AI, interception |
| `src/shop.js` | the upgrades, described as data (`upgrades`, `shopLayout`), and the shop |
| `src/menu.js` | the pause menu, built from the upgrades: sections, weapon tabs, the lines of the weapons' status and mode, tooltips |
| `src/tiles.js` | the upgrades shown as tiles in the pause menu: their pictures, their values written over them, the color of their frame, and their tooltips telling what a click buys |
| `src/achievements.js` | the achievements, described as data (`achievements`), their medals, their saving, their announcement and their page |
| `src/researches.js` | the research tree, described as data (`researchBranches`, `researches`): the research going on, its section of the pause menu, and its note at the bottom right |
| `src/adventure.js` | the Adventure, described as data (`adventureLevels`): each level's waves, what it brings to the pause menu, its challenge, its stars saved by the browser, and its end screen (hidden from the start menu until it is ready, `adventureReady`, but shown when the game is opened as `index.html?adventure`) |
| `src/bonuses.js` | the luck the pause menu can raise: the critical shots and their flashes, the asteroids with a metal (`metals` in `src/parameters.js`), the veins on their rock and the worth of their debris |
| `src/threats.js` | the threats coming one more every two minutes in the Arcade: the NAZA's announces (message, countdown, mat given, the side marked), the comets, the splitting asteroids, the swarms and the armored asteroids |
| `src/starshop.js` | the bonuses of the Adventure, bought with its stars (hidden with the Adventure) |
| `src/menuscenes.js` | the scenes behind the start menu's two pages: the Earth rising, the rain of asteroids (hidden with the Adventure) |
| `src/sound.js` | the sounds, made by the browser itself (Web Audio), with no file: off until the player turns them on |
| `src/secondary.js` | the secondary weapons, which take no place among the active ones: the missile launcher, its missiles and their blasts; the nitroglycerine launcher, its capsules and its bombs; the laser and its beam; the rebound cannon, its shots and the sparks of their ricochets, and the ways the Trick Shot foresees |
| `src/kamikaze.js` | the kamikazes: sent with a click on an asteroid or with F, their way ahead of their asteroid to ram it head-on, their building, and their gauge at the bottom left |
| `src/cryo.js` | the Cryo cannon: its ice shards flying to the asteroids, the asteroids frozen, then thawed where they are, and their frost |
| `src/hammer.js` | the Hammer & Broom's waves: growing from the planet up to their reach, damaging and slowing down the asteroids they cross, and drawn translucent, the sky rippling through them |
| `src/gameover.js` | the Game Over screen (the score, the game's numbers, the records saved and beaten, the NAZA's tip chosen from the game, `lossTips`, the achievements of the game) and the hi-scores page |
| `src/objects.js`, `src/prototypes.js` | game objects and their drawing: the turning Earth and the asteroids' rocks |
| `img/earth.js` | the Earth's map, written as text: a browser opening `index.html` from the disk would not let the game read the pixels of an image file |
| `img/stars.js` | the stars of the game area's sky, found once in its picture, for the game to make them twinkle |
| `src/parameters.js` | settings, the starting values that tune the difficulty (`startValues`), and what each difficulty level changes (`difficultyLevels`) |

Adding an upgrade only takes a line in `upgrades` and its place in `shopLayout`. The game uses no library: the pause menu used jQuery 1.4.3, jQuery UI 1.8.5 and three plugins until 0.10.1.
